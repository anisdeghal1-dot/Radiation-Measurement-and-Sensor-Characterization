# Radiation Measurement and Sensor Characterization

## Overview
This project investigates optical radiation measurement using a BPW34 photodiode and an Ocean Optics spectrometer.

## Objectives
- Characterize two optical sensing technologies.
- Study the relationship between light intensity and source distance.
- Perform sensor calibration and analyze measurement uncertainty.
- Compare measurements obtained with a halogen lamp and a UV source.

## Experimental Setup
- **Photodiode:** BPW34, operated in photovoltaic mode.
- **Spectrometer:** Ocean Optics spectrometer with optical fiber.
- **Light sources:** Halogen lamp and UV generator.
- **Acquisition:** Digital multimeter and OceanView software.

## Methods
- Photodiode voltage measurements at different distances and orientations.
- Spectral intensity measurements using an optical fiber and spectrometer.
- Calibration based on reference measurements.
- Type A and Type B uncertainty estimation.
- Comparison and interpretation of experimental results.

## Key Findings
- Photodiode measurements were consistent with the inverse-square law over the studied range.
- Sensor orientation significantly affected the measured voltage.
- The spectrometer provided wavelength-dependent information that complemented the photodiode measurements.
- Measurement limitations and uncertainty sources were examined.

## Tools and Equipment
